# Open Factor intro film: three storylines

The one thing: **logging a workout is quick and calm, the app is free, and your training stays on your phone.**
Facts used (from the README and the app): 1,300+ exercises, demonstration videos for 619 of them, the in-app keypad, swipe to log, rest timer, history, five color themes, no account, works offline, free and open source (MIT).

Shared assumptions: 40–50 s, 1920×1080, 30 fps, no voiceover, music plus a few UI sounds, real screen recordings from the app on an emulator loaded with realistic demo training history.

---

## A. One Set (recommended)

**Device:** one real set travels through the whole app. **Form:** one continuous take; the camera never cuts, it pushes and pulls through the phone.

The viewer follows a single set, *100 kg × 5 on Bench Press*, from the moment it is typed to the week it becomes part of. It feels like the quiet focus between sets, not a feature tour.

- **First 2 s:** a tight crop of the real set row. The keypad's digits tap in "5", the row swipes right, the check lands. A large line arrives beside it: *One more set*.
- **Beats:** set logged (hook) → rest timer counts down, "what did I lift last time?" (tension) → the floating navigation slides up and the title *Open Factor* lands (reveal) → the camera follows the set: the "Last time" note, Guide opens and the demonstration plays with its muscle map, Swap suggests an alternative → the camera pulls out to History and the Dashboard rings fill with the week (payoff) → *Free. No account. Yours.* (claim) → end card.
- **Look:** the app's own charcoal (#121212) and white, DM Sans, the phone shown full-height and large; a single soft rim light as the one material. Accent is rationed to the check mark and the end card.
- **Sound:** a calm, warm minimal pulse around 90 bpm that holds back until the dashboard payoff, then opens; soft taps on keypad presses, one swipe whoosh, one pop on the check.

## B. Every Rep, Shown

**Device:** the result first. **Form:** an endless zoom.

It opens on a demonstration video playing full frame, then pulls back to reveal it inside Open Factor's exercise guide. The camera dives into the muscle map; a highlighted muscle becomes the next world (the exercise library filtered to it), and so on. Learning the movement is the hero.

- **First 2 s:** a full-frame demonstration plays at 1X, the speed control taps to 2.5X, and the frame begins to pull back.
- **Beats:** video playing → pull back into the guide → dive into the anatomy map → the library of 1,300+ exercises scrolls → pick one, log it → end card.
- **Look:** the demonstration footage carries the color; UI in charcoal frames; very large type.
- **Sound:** an airy, rising ambient track with a single hit when the camera exits the anatomy map.
- **Note:** this one leans heavily on the exercise videos and photos. Their ownership is separate from the app's code license, so confirm you have the right to use them in a public promo first.

## C. Five Colors (the bold one)

**Device:** type carries the argument. **Form:** cut on the beat, with match cuts through the five color themes.

Giant DM Sans words fill the frame on solid color fields: *No account. No ads. No subscription.* Each cut lands on a real screen of the app re-colored in the next theme (Charcoal, Midnight, Ember, Moss, Mono), so the color field and the app become the same thing.

- **First 2 s:** a full-bleed Ember field with *No account* in huge type; on the beat the field shrinks into the Ember-themed Workout screen.
- **Beats:** three statements, each a palette → the keypad, swipe and history shown fast in their themes → all five phones line up and collapse into one → end card.
- **Look:** flat color fields straight from the app's palettes, oversized DM Sans, hard cuts.
- **Sound:** a punchy 120 bpm track; cuts land on the beat, no other effects.
